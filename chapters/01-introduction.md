# Introduction: Overview of the NYC Volunteer Fire Department Project

In this course, students examine the volunteer fire departments that
serve mainly New York City's coastal and geographically isolated
communities. These volunteer fire departments must balance public safety
responsibilities with financial sustainability. All of them are nonprofit
organizations that serve as instructive experiential learning
environments, facing accounting and financial management issues such as
replacing major emergency equipment — which can cost hundreds of
thousands of dollars and is typically funded through grants, fundraising,
and financing.

In this project, students build a six-year financial dataset covering
all NYC volunteer firehouse nonprofits and analyze their financial
sustainability using Excel. Students also visit a local firehouse to
understand its operations and equipment needs, informing their analysis.
Students simulate funding scenarios and assess the risk and financial
readiness of these organizations to make significant equipment and other
purchases.

## History of New York City Volunteer Fire Departments

Volunteer fire departments began playing an increasingly important role
in New York City's fire service in the late 1800s. Facing inadequate
funding, poor equipment, and limited training opportunities, a group of
volunteer firefighters formed the **Volunteer Firemen's Association of
the City of New York (VFACNY)** to advocate for the needs of volunteer
fire departments and provide support and resources to its members.

Today, there are eight volunteer fire departments across NYC working
alongside the FDNY.

## NYC Volunteer Fire Departments Offer an Experience to Connect Theory to Practice

Many accounting and finance students report difficulty connecting
abstract financial theories to real-world decision-making, particularly
in organizations that do not operate for profit. Traditional coursework
often relies on short case studies offering limited insight into how
organizations function in practice. Compared to other fields (education,
healthcare, physical sciences), accounting and finance programs offer
relatively few opportunities for immersive, community-based experiences.

This project addresses that gap by placing students directly in the
context of NYC volunteer fire departments, where financial decisions are
closely tied to public safety, community trust, and resource
constraints. Unlike traditional business environments, volunteer fire
departments rely on a combination of grants, donations, fundraising
efforts, and limited public funding to sustain operations.

Rather than analyzing a static case, students develop an evolving
understanding of how accounting and financial management issues function
in a high-stakes, community-centered environment — exploring questions
such as: *How does a fire department fund a new apparatus? What
trade-offs are made between equipment upgrades and community outreach?
How do financial constraints influence emergency readiness?*

## Project Implementation

Throughout the semester, students study accounting and financial
management concepts through assigned readings and course materials. This
project complements those materials with a structured opportunity to
apply theory in a real business context.

The project consists of **five deliverables** spaced across the
semester. Students collect nonprofit tax return data on all NYC
volunteer fire departments and analyze it, identify and visit a local
fire department, analyze risk and funding scenarios, prepare a
presentation on findings, and reflect on how the experience developed
employer-desired competencies.

:::{admonition} Research Study Information
:class: note
Outcomes of the deliverables may be analyzed to contribute to an
educational research study. Students will be invited to consent
separately; all identifying information is de-identified, and any
photographs used for research purposes (e.g., conference presentations)
will have names anonymized. Only the principal investigator and
co-investigators will have access, and outcomes are stored on a
password-protected, cloud-based server.
:::
