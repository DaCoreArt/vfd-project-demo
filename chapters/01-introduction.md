# Introduction: Overview of the NYC Volunteer Fire Department Project

In this course, students examine the volunteer fire departments that
serve mainly New York City's coastal and geographically isolated
communities. These volunteer fire departments must balance public safety
responsibilities with financial sustainability. All of them are nonprofit
organizations that serve as instructive experiential learning
environments. These departments face accounting and financial management
issues, such as replacing major emergency equipment, which can cost
hundreds of thousands of dollars and is typically funded through grants,
fundraising, and financing.

In this project, students build a six-year financial dataset covering
all NYC volunteer firehouse nonprofits and analyze their financial
sustainability using Excel. Students also visit a local firehouse to
understand its operations and equipment needs, which will inform their
analysis. Students simulate funding scenarios and assess the risk and
financial readiness of these organizations to make significant equipment
and other purchases.

## History of New York City Volunteer Fire Departments

Per The Volunteer Firemen's Association of the City of New York (2025)
(VFACNY), volunteer fire departments began to play an increasingly
important role in the fire service in New York City in the late 1800s. At
that time, the city's volunteer fire departments were facing a number of
challenges, including inadequate funding, poor equipment, and a lack of
training opportunities. In response, a group of volunteer firefighters
formed the VFACNY in order to advocate for the needs of volunteer fire
departments and to provide support and resources to its members
[@vfacny2025].

The VFACNY was established to advocate for the rights and interests of
volunteer firefighters, and to provide them with training, equipment and
other resources. The organization's early leaders worked to improve the
conditions and status of volunteer firefighters in New York City.

Over the years, the VFACNY has played a key role in advocating for better
working conditions, and benefits for volunteer firefighters. It has also
worked to improve the quality and availability of training for volunteer
firefighters and to increase funding for volunteer fire departments.

In addition to advocating for the interests of volunteer fire departments,
the VFACNY has also played an important role in promoting the history and
tradition of the fire service in New York City. The organization has
organized events and ceremonies to commemorate the contributions of
volunteer firefighters, and has worked to preserve the history and
heritage of the fire service in the city. Throughout its history, the
VFACNY has played an important role in supporting volunteer firefighters
in New York City, and it continues to be a vital organization for the
thousands of men and women who volunteer their time and expertise to
protect their communities from the threat of fire. They work alongside
career firefighters and provide vital services to residents and visitors.

Despite these changes, the VFACNY and its departments play an important
part of the fire service in New York City. Today, there are 8 volunteer
fire departments across the city that work alongside the New York City
Fire Department (FDNY), which is one of the largest fire departments in
the world, staffed by over 11,000 career firefighters [@vfacny2025]. The
eight fire departments are: Broad Channel VFD & Ambulance Corp.,
Edgewater Park Volunteer Hose Company No. 1, Gerritsen Beach Volunteer
Fire Department, Point Breeze Volunteer Fire Department, Richmond Engine
Company #1, Rockaway Point Volunteer Fire Department, Roxbury Volunteer
Fire Department, and West Hamilton Beach Volunteer Fire Department.

These volunteer departments provide vital services to residents and
visitors, and are staffed by men and women who are dedicated to protecting
their communities from the threat of fire and other emergencies. Today,
the VFACNY continues to play an important role in supporting and
representing the interests of volunteer fire departments in New York City.
It continues to work closely with the FDNY and other organizations to
ensure that volunteer departments have the resources they need to protect
the communities they serve.

## NYC Volunteer Fire Departments Offer an Experience to Connect Theory to Practice

Many accounting and finance students report difficulty connecting
abstract financial theories to real-world decision-making, particularly
in organizations that do not operate for profit. Traditional coursework
often relies on short case studies that offer limited insight into how
organizations function in practice. Compared to other fields (e.g.,
education, healthcare, and the physical sciences), accounting and finance
programs offer relatively few opportunities for immersive,
community-based experiences in both undergraduate and graduate settings
[@foy2024; @foy2025]. This gap is especially evident when students
attempt to understand how financial management operates within
mission-driven organizations such as volunteer fire departments.

This project addresses that gap by placing students directly in the
context of New York City volunteer fire departments, where financial
decisions are closely tied to public safety, community trust, and
resource constraints. Unlike traditional business environments, volunteer
fire departments rely on a combination of grants, donations, fundraising
efforts, and limited public funding to sustain operations. Through this
immersive, semester-long venue-based experiential learning project,
students engage with real organizations, observing how financial
decisions are made, how funding priorities are established, and how
operational needs (e.g., equipment, training, facilities) are balanced
against financial realities.

Rather than analyzing a static case, students develop an evolving
understanding of how accounting and financial management issues function
in a high-stakes, community-centered environment. We explore questions
such as: How does a fire department fund a new apparatus? What trade-offs
are made between equipment upgrades and community outreach? How do
financial constraints influence emergency readiness? This sustained
engagement allows students to connect theoretical concepts, such as
budgeting, capital financing, and cost analysis, to real-world
implications that directly impact public safety.

By the end of the project, students are expected to develop a deeper,
more practical understanding of how financial principles apply in
nonprofit and public service contexts. In addition, the experience
supports the development of transferable, employer-valued skills such as
communication, critical thinking, adaptability, and professional
judgment, particularly in environments where financial decisions carry
significant social consequences.

The instructions in this document are designed to guide students in
linking textbook concepts to real-world observations and analysis within
their local communities. This project encourages students to view
accounting and finance not simply as technical disciplines, but as
essential tools for supporting organizations that serve and protect the
public. This manual is intentionally flexible and may be refined as
needed. Students and instructors are encouraged to provide feedback and
identify opportunities for clarification or enhancement as the project
evolves.

## Project Implementation

Throughout the semester, students study accounting and financial
management concepts through assigned readings and course materials. This
project complements those materials by providing a structured opportunity
to apply theory in a real business context.

The project consists of five deliverables spaced across the semester.
Students collect nonprofit tax return data on all NYC volunteer fire
departments and analyze them, identify and visit a local fire department
to familiarize themselves with fire department apparatus, analyze risk
and funding scenarios, prepare a presentation on findings, and reflect on
how the experience developed employer-desired competencies.

While all deliverables are graded individually, collaboration by sharing
experiences with fellow classmates is highly encouraged. This structure
reflects real workplace environments, where individuals are evaluated on
both independent performance and share experiences with others.

:::{admonition} Research Study Information
:class: note
Even though this project is required to fulfill course objectives,
outcomes of the deliverables may be analyzed to contribute to an
educational research study. At the end of the project, students may
receive an email invitation to take a survey related to the research
asking for consent to use project outcomes. If students agree, all
student work is de-identified. Identifying information (for example, IP
addresses, student ID) is initially collected but later removed. Upon
review of outcomes, a separate request may ask written permission to
disclose certain outcomes, such as pictures taken during the project. All
names are anonymized. Any photograph used will be used for research
purposes, such as presentations at conferences or publication. The
principal investigator and the co-investigators will be the only parties
with access. Outcomes will be stored in a password-protected cloud-based
server.
:::

## Final Thoughts

In addition to the instructions set forth in this guide, to enhance
understanding of the business entity and its environment, students should
use all resources available to them, including but not limited to the
library (specifically the business librarian), Internet, professional
opinions and interpretations, company published information, brokerage
house information, and similar sources. Students should think about
creative ways to extract and understand information. This instruction
guide will direct students, but it will not hold their hand during the
semester. It is the student's project to learn from.

Good luck with this experiential learning project and let it enrich your
understanding of accounting and corporate finance!
