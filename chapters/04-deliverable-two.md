# Deliverable Two: Financial Analysis and Firehouse Visitation

**Course Learning Outcomes:** CLO 2, CLO 4, CLO 6

## Project Learning Outcomes

1. Integrate employer-desired competencies to develop highly valued
   employer-desired skills.
2. Construct and analyze a comprehensive financial profile of a
   nonprofit organization using real-world data and field-based
   observations.
3. Analyze real nonprofit financial data and interpret nonprofit
   operations from a nonprofit accounting and financial management
   perspective.

Deliverable two is intended to allow students to engage in higher-level
financial analysis of nonprofit organizations and to immerse themselves
in a venue-based, concrete experience. Deliverable two helps connect the
abstract theories you are learning in the course materials with active
experimentation by researching data on a real-life scenario and
evaluating feasibilities.

## Task D2.1 — Review and Analyze Financial Statements

Using the Excel spreadsheet developed in deliverable one, students
analyze the financial data and calculate key financial metrics for all
available years, such as:

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Operating Surplus/Deficit | Total Revenue − Total Expenses |
| Operating Margin | Operating Surplus / Total Revenue |
| Reserve Ratio | Cash / Total Expenses |
| Liability Burden | Liabilities / Total Revenue |
| Donation Dependence | Donations / Total Revenue |

## Task D2.2 — Firehouse Visitation

In experiential learning, this is considered a venue-based, concrete
task. Students conduct a field visit to a local Firehouse. A concrete
experience is what you directly observe when you visit the firehouse,
such as the fire truck apparatus, protective gear, and communications
equipment. The concrete experience is designed to help you understand
abstract concepts in your assigned readings.

The purpose of the visit is to engage your external sensory perceptions,
such as seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting (if applicable), and
experiencing physical sensations such as touch, as well as your internal
body's natural awareness of movement, position, effort, balance, fatigue,
emotions, all known as kinesthetic proprioception, as you observe a local
firehouse in action.

### Why is engaging in your senses important for learning?

Learning is deeper and more meaningful when it involves the whole body,
not just the eyes and the mind. By paying attention to how work feels,
such as the pace of movement, the physical effort required, the tightness
of space, and the rhythm of tasks, these senses help you gain insights
that cannot be captured by numbers alone. This bodily awareness helps you
understand why accounting and finance outcomes appear as they do. Labor
costs, productivity, errors, waste, pricing, and staffing decisions are
all influenced by the physical realities of work. When you notice strain,
repetition, smooth coordination, or congestion, you are observing the
real forces that shape costs and revenues.

Engaging your senses and bodily awareness during the visit is intended to
set the stage for you to connect abstract financial concepts to real
operational experience, thereby helping you transform accounting and
financial management from theoretical to practical applications and to
understand how a nonprofit business actually functions and generates
revenue and incurs expenses.

:::{admonition} Virtual option
:class: tip
For those with an accommodation letter who cannot physically visit a
local firehouse, there is a virtual option to complete this task.
Students with accessibility limitations should rely on Google Maps, Yelp,
a local firehouse's website, or other means such as online reviews,
images, and interview people who may have a close connection to the local
firehouse chosen. You can also work with classmates who may be able to
FaceTime their visitation live so you can share the experience. This
option has been done successfully in prior coursework as a virtual
option.
:::

Students must visit one local firehouse (either volunteer or
government-run) to learn about its basic operations and equipment needs.
Firefighters are generally very friendly and love to talk about anything
related to firehouses. During the visit, students should attempt to
discuss:

- Equipment that needs replacement (e.g., fire trucks, drones,
  ambulances, etc.)
- Construction or infrastructure needs (e.g., parking lots, firehouse
  renovations)
- Funding sources (if a volunteer firehouse — donations, grants, etc.)
- Recruitment and training needs

## Task D2.3 — Reflection

After you have completed your financial analysis and visitation, in two
to three paragraphs, describe how your skills and competencies have been
strengthened by engaging in Deliverable Two. Specifically, reflect on how
you developed a deeper understanding and application in key areas such as
financial reasoning, problem-solving, curiosity, adaptability, critical
thinking, empathy, emotional intelligence, and written and oral
communication. How did it help you grow and develop your ability to
engage in nonprofit accounting and financial management analysis and
understand the complex operations of a nonprofit organization?
