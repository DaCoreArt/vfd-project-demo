# Deliverable Four: Communicating Results

**Course Learning Outcomes:** CLO 1 through CLO 6

## Project Learning Outcomes

1. Integrate employer-desired competencies to develop highly valued
   employer-desired skills.
2. Construct and analyze a comprehensive financial profile of a
   nonprofit organization using real-world data and field-based
   observations.
3. Analyze real nonprofit financial data and interpret nonprofit
   operations from a nonprofit accounting and financial management
   perspective.
4. Defend evidence-based financial recommendations under realistic
   operational constraints in a nonprofit context.
5. Communicate financial insights and reflect on experiential learning
   to demonstrate professional judgment, analytical thinking, and
   applied competency development.

Deliverable Four challenges students to synthesize and present a
nonprofit accounting and financial management story developed over
deliverables one, two, and three. This should be presented in a concise,
professional PowerPoint. Deliverable four not only reinforces key
theories learned during the semester but also deepens the real-world
application of financial reasoning in evaluating and advising on
nonprofit accounting and financial management issues. Deliverable four is
also designed to cultivate and demonstrate high-demand professional
competencies aligned with Deloitte's Workforce of the Future.

This individually graded assignment emphasizes resilience and
accountability, requiring students to rehearse, communicate regularly,
and produce polished, high-stakes output under a collaborative deadline.
By the end of this process, students will have developed a more holistic
skill set—blending technical finance expertise with interpersonal,
digital communication abilities critical for future business
environments.

During the semester, students are encouraged to draft slides and edit
them as they complete deliverables one, two, and three.

## How to Make a PowerPoint Slide Show

### D4 Requirements

1. Draft a storyboard to convey it in a concise presentation, which can
   simply be a series of empty slides in a PowerPoint that students
   populate with their ideas. Perhaps a simple and effective
   demonstration of how to present a storyboard is by Leila Gharani, a
   prominent YouTuber who specializes in Microsoft product
   demonstrations. She presents information clearly and concisely. She is
   not the only person who specializes in this space, but she is well
   known with several million subscribers. She has produced a video that
   covers screen recording and recording a slideshow. Students are not
   bound to use this storyboard demonstration. Students are encouraged to
   pursue other methods if they are easier or workable.

2. The whole slideshow presentation should be no longer than five
   minutes. There is no minimum or maximum amount of slides. Just fall
   within the time limit. Targeting about 10 slides is suggested.

The late policy for deliverable four is the same as for prior
deliverables. It is important that students provide their presentations
in a timely manner, as it is a near-end-of-semester submission.

:::{note}
I cannot stress enough to students how important it is to rehearse
presentations, so mistakes are minimized during the recording and have
proper timing. If a student experiences physical challenges speaking
clearly, consult the Office of Accommodations for alternatives such as
closed captioning.
:::
